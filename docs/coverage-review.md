# Where the learning journey stands

Reviewed October 20, 2026. Math Atlas now has **207 distinct adventure missions, 621 challenges, and 91 teaching concept families**. The original **80-lesson visual library** remains available alongside the adventure.

The strongest connected sequence was Grade 3. The largest immediate gap was the path leading into it. This expansion adds **24 original lessons each for Kindergarten, Grade 1, and Grade 2**: 72 lessons and 216 challenges. Each young learner can answer with controls or choices. Two older activities containing arithmetic beyond ten were moved out of Kindergarten's placement.

## What the connected early years provide

The [primary-years guide](primary-years.md) lays out six units per grade. The [Grade 3 guide](grade-3-year.md) lays out eight. Each year offers about 36 flexible study weeks, short worked examples, prerequisites, explanation prompts, and suggested review. These budgets help a family revisit school topics; they are not 180 daily lesson plans.

The learning progression moves from objects and actions to pictures, quantities, and symbols. Kindergarten joins and removes small collections. Grade 1 develops addition and subtraction strategies and tens. Grade 2 extends bundles, measurement, time, data, arrays, and equal shares. Grade 3 develops multiplication, division, and fractions as numbers. Correctly finishing a short mission records practice, not mastery of a grade or standard.

Continue deepening these years with longer discussions, additional representations, and problems that do not closely resemble the worked example. Kindergarten still needs more practice with the counting sequence through 100, numeral writing away from a screen, and varied number combinations. Grades 1–2 need sustained untimed arithmetic practice, more varied unknown positions and measurement situations, and classroom transfer. Grade 3 needs continued review and richer applications rather than only larger inventories of similar questions.

## Build upward in this order

| Priority | Main gaps to fill | A useful visual direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grade 4 | Multi-digit calculation, deeper fraction work, decimal fractions, measurement conversions, angles, and symmetry | Trade place-value bundles, partition area models, and connect equivalent fractions on a fixed line |
| Grade 5 | Fraction and decimal operations, division, measurement conversions, coordinates, and a connected volume sequence | Keep the whole and unit clear while connecting fraction bars, decimal grids, sharing, and cube layers |
| Grades 6–8 | Complete rational-number, expression, equation, geometry, statistics, and probability paths with review | Link number lines, balance models, tables, graphs, shape transformations, and data experiments |
| High school | Connected course paths, proof, modeling, broader symbolic work, and statistics | Connect equations with graphs and geometric or data-based reasons; retain mature presentation options |
| College | Foundations and prerequisites before expanding the current sampler | Make precise model assumptions and connect a picture with its formal notation |

The proposed Grade 4 and Grade 5 priorities draw on their official [Grade 4 overview](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/4/introduction/) and [Grade 5 overview](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/5/introduction/). Middle-school planning references are the [Grade 6](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/6/introduction/), [Grade 7](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/7/introduction/), and [Grade 8](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/8/introduction/) overviews.

## Evidence to require as content grows

- State the learning goal, prerequisite, worked reasoning, and what each visible quantity counts.
- Introduce an unfamiliar idea before checking an answer; keep hints and adult guidance available.
- Verify answers against independent worked examples and check that controls preserve the intended mathematics.
- Check keyboard, narrow-screen, reading-support, and reduced-motion use. Young learners should not need typing to demonstrate an idea.
- Include varied contexts and representations, then ask whether the learner can explain a fresh example.
- Record exact source editions and licenses for adaptations. The new 72 activities are original; the four existing credited IM adaptations are unchanged.

These are development and review criteria. They do not imply educator endorsement, verified state-by-state alignment, or a particular publisher's lesson order. Teacher accounts, classroom tracking, and voice narration remain outside the current student-first scope.
