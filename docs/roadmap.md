# Where Math Atlas can grow

Math Atlas currently offers 80 interactive lessons in nine collections. This roadmap describes useful directions, not promised releases or completed features. Feedback and small contributions help decide what comes next.

## Student adventure comes first

The student release adds selected missions across six learning stages, original woodland companions, visual challenges, and optional local rewards. Younger learners use game interactions; later stages connect the same approach with typed answers, equations, and graphs. The [adventure guide](adventure.md) explains the current scope and saved progress.

The next priority is to observe whether students understand the models, can use the controls independently, and can apply an idea in a different problem. Expand content and refine rewards from that feedback. Clocks, number sense, fractions, geometry, algebra, and introductory college explorations need continued student and educator review before any claim of complete curriculum coverage.

Teacher dashboards, accounts, classroom assignments, shared student records, and hosted progress services are deliberately deferred while the student experience is tested. No classroom backend is included in this release.

## Help students carry an idea into a new problem

- **Guided word problems:** connect a story to a diagram, identify quantities and units, build an equation, and check whether the answer makes sense.
- **Richer practice:** ask several different questions about one idea, vary representations, and distinguish a lucky correct answer from a clear explanation.
- **Misconception-aware hints:** address common errors using a useful counterexample or a different picture.
- **More prerequisite links:** make it easier to back up one step without losing the original learning goal.
- **Worked comparisons:** show how the same principle applies to a familiar and an unfamiliar example.

## Deepen coverage

This edition is an introduction to major topic families. It does not yet fully cover:

- Extended geometric proofs, compass-and-straightedge constructions, and proof writing.
- Longer, multi-step application and modeling sequences.
- Comprehensive operations on polynomial and rational expressions.
- Full treatment of statistical inference, study design, sampling, and uncertainty.
- A complete precalculus or calculus sequence, including differential equations and infinite series.
- Full assessments or reviewed state-by-state standards alignment.

The typed-equation helper is intentionally limited to the documented `ax + b = c` range. Broader equation input would need a carefully bounded parser, useful explanations, and checks for ambiguous or unsupported input.

## Improve access and teaching confidence

- Review lessons with students, educators, keyboard users, and screen-reader users.
- Improve descriptions of diagrams and alternative representations.
- Refine narrow-screen layouts, reading settings, contrast, and reduced-motion behavior.
- Add reviewed translations without losing mathematical precision.
- Map lessons to specific standards with clear evidence and educator review before claiming alignment.

The current reading presets support individual preferences. Any future claims about learning or accessibility outcomes need evidence; a setting alone is not a clinical intervention.

## Make visuals earn their place

Anatomy-powered isometric views currently support six measurement and solid-geometry lessons. New three-dimensional views should clarify a spatial relationship, preserve truthful scales and units, and have a useful flat or textual companion. Other ideas may be clearer as a number line, graph, table, or area model.

Potential additions include nets that connect to solid faces, cross-sections, geometric transformations, and visual proofs. Decorative depth should never obscure what the student is trying to learn.

## Keep the project easy to run and trust

- Keep the published app self-contained and usable without sign-in.
- Expand meaningful math-model, input, and packaging checks as new features are added.
- Make browser and accessibility verification repeatable.
- Preserve documented limits and third-party credits.
- Discuss export, shared-device profiles, or sync separately before changing how student data is stored.

## Tell us what would help

Use [the feedback templates](https://github.com/dancinglumberjack/math-atlas/issues/new/choose) and include:

1. The lesson or concept.
2. Where the explanation or interaction stopped helping.
3. A sample problem or proposed improvement.
4. How you would know the change made the idea easier to understand.

Please keep reports anonymous and omit student identities, school details, diagnoses, and grades.
