# Growing from Kindergarten through Grade 2

[Open the adventure](https://dancinglumberjack.github.io/math-atlas/adventure.html) · [Grade 3 next steps](grade-3-year.md) · [Current coverage and gaps](coverage-review.md)

Choose **Kindergarten**, **Grade 1**, or **Grade 2** and **all topics** to follow six connected units. Choose a single topic for a shorter homework route. Kindergarten now offers **39 activities**, Grade 1 **36**, and Grade 2 **37**, including earlier activities placed within the new paths. Each grade adds 24 original lessons and 72 challenges.

Every year suggests six flexible weeks per unit: about 36 weeks altogether. Spend more or less time on an idea, revisit an earlier lesson, or explore the next unit whenever useful. A short mission is one piece of practice, rather than a complete day's instruction. Explore with a grown-up who can read, point, and ask a gentle question.

## Kindergarten: make the number visible

| Unit | Explore | Try away from the screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Little collections and one more | One count per object, zero, the next number, and a collection that keeps its count when moved | Count spoons, rearrange them, and check whether any arrived or left |
| 2. A full ten and extra friends | Ten-frame spaces, teen numbers, and counting on from a known amount | Build one full group of ten buttons and some extra ones |
| 3. Compare counts and find the parts | Matching collections, comparing numerals, splitting five, and completing ten | Share five counters between two bowls in several ways |
| 4. Join, take away, and tell the story | Arriving guests, joined parts, disappearing objects, and a missing part | Act out a joining or leaving story with small toys |
| 5. Compare, sort, and count | Length and height from a shared start, shape categories, and their counts | Line up pencils and sort a few paper shapes |
| 6. Shape friends and little builders | Flat and solid shapes, turns, positions, and shapes made from smaller pieces | Find a ball, box, and can; join two paper squares into a rectangle |

Give objects meaning before asking for a numeral. Touch one object for each number name, then point to the collection when saying the final count. Show that moving the objects does not change the count. The official [Kindergarten introduction](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/introduction/) informed this focus on quantities, small operations, and shapes.

## Grade 1: find helpful ways to combine and separate

| Unit | Explore | Try away from the screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Numbers on the woodland trail | Count from a chosen number, build teens and tens, and read quantities through 120 | Bundle counters into tens and name the loose ones |
| 2. Joining groups with helpful strategies | Count on, make ten, use doubles, and combine three small groups | Find a friendly ten in 8 + 5 with counters |
| 3. Taking apart and finding a missing piece | Take away, bridge through ten, use related facts, and find a missing starting amount | Hide part of a collection and check by joining it back |
| 4. Think in tens | Compare two-digit numbers, add or remove tens, and trade ten loose ones | Build ten more and ten less than a chosen number |
| 5. Lengths, votes, and friendly clocks | Measure with equal units, order lengths, read three categories, and set hours and half-hours | Measure with equal paper squares and move a paper clock |
| 6. Shapes and fair shares | Defining sides and corners, composing shapes, equal halves, and quarters | Fold matching rectangles and compare one half with one quarter |

For **8 + 5**, move two of the five counters to the eight to fill ten. Three remain: **10 + 3 = 13**. Nothing was added by regrouping. For **13 − 5**, remove three to reach ten, then two more to reach eight. Explain which counters moved at each step. This progression connects with the [Grade 1 introduction](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/introduction/).

## Grade 2: extend the same ideas to bigger quantities

| Unit | Explore | Try away from the screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Hundreds, names, and number trails | Build and compare three-digit numbers and skip-count by fives, tens, and hundreds | Make number cards and arrange them by place value |
| 2. Trade bundles and tell the story | Regroup to add or subtract, work within 1,000, and follow two story steps | Use paper bundles to explain 28 + 17 and 52 − 27 |
| 3. Buddies, equal rows, and information | Odd and even, arrays, turned rows, and single-unit graphs | Arrange snacks in equal rows, then turn the tray |
| 4. Measure, compare, and mark a line | Whole-unit rulers, length differences, number-line moves, and line plots | Measure paper strips in centimeters and plot every length |
| 5. Clock hops and the pretend shop | Five-minute times, a.m./p.m., U.S. coin values, and change | Set a clock for a daily event and run a paper-coin shop |
| 6. Shape rules, square tiles, and fair shares | Shape attributes, equal square rows, halves, thirds, and fourths | Fold equal rectangles in different directions and compare the shares |

Keep the unit attached to the number. A ten-bundle is ten ones; a ruler interval has a stated length; a dime is ten cents; a plot mark stands for one measured object. Name that unit before calculating. The [Grade 2 introduction](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/2/introduction/) informed the emphasis on place value, calculation, measurement, and shape reasoning.

## A small learning loop

1. Meet the idea in the visual introduction. Move through the cards at your own pace.
2. Build or inspect the picture. Ask, “What does this number count?”
3. Use the large controls or tap a choice, then check when ready. No K–2 challenge requires a typed answer.
4. Open **Our lesson** for a worked example and an explanation or review prompt. Use a hint whenever useful.
5. Try the idea with real objects or a different small example before moving on.

The screen stays silent. No speed score or lost life is attached to a mistake. Reading support, larger text, spacing, reduced motion, and a quieter presentation remain available. The object action comes first; equations and notation explain that same action as learners are ready for them.

These independently authored paths support schoolwork and adult guidance. They have a small bounded set of replay examples and do not replace sustained arithmetic practice, handwriting, classroom discussion, or a teacher's assessment.
