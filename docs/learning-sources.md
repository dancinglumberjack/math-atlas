# Open lessons and connections to schoolwork

Math Atlas has 99 authored missions and 297 challenges, alongside its original 80-lesson visual library. Grade and topic filters help a family find the idea being taught at school. This is a growing practice collection, not a complete grade course or independently certified standards alignment. Review can appear under more than one grade, and high-school sequences vary.

## Grade 3 multiplication and division

The new focused collection adds twelve multiplication missions and twelve division missions. Each has three challenges, introductory visual teaching, help, and a small bounded set of replay examples. This count represents distinct authored activities; changing the numbers does not count as another lesson.

Multiplication connects equal groups, arrays, repeated addition, skip-counting, reordered factors, zero and one, missing factors, split arrays, doubling, and groups of ten. Division distinguishes **how many in each share** from **how many groups**, connects inverse facts, reconstructs a missing total or divisor, uses backward jumps, and checks a quotient by multiplying. Short two-step situations come after these basic meanings.

These activities connect to [Grade 3 Operations and Algebraic Thinking](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/3/OA/), particularly interpreting products and quotients, equal-group problems, unknown factors, properties, and related multiplication/division facts. They support parts of these standards; a single short activity does not establish fluency or cover an entire standard. They do not require a timed facts test. A learner may use counters and written reasoning.

At this stage, build the picture and name the quantities before introducing a written shortcut. For later multi-digit work, connect a place-value or area model to partial products and partial quotients before expecting an unexplained algorithm. Grade 4's original classroom lessons below provide a next step; they have not been imported into the game.

## Expanding carefully

For each future adaptation, record the exact edition, lesson URL, author, license, changes, prerequisites, grade/topic connection, and independently worked answer checks. Review the visual model against the written method, try it with learners, and keep attribution beside the activity. A new representation or learning goal is a new activity; renamed stories and random numbers alone are not curriculum growth.

License terms differ by edition and website. The newer IM v.360 edition is noncommercial, and current OpenStax Prealgebra and the older IM task bank carry noncommercial/share-alike terms. Those materials were not imported into this release. Future contributions must check the specific page rather than assuming all resources from a publisher share the same terms.
