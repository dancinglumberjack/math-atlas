# Grades 4–5: grow the picture with the mathematics

Choose **Grade 4** or **Grade 5**, then **all topics**, on the [adventure map](https://dancinglumberjack.github.io/math-atlas/adventure.html#map). Each grade has eight connected units across about 36 flexible study weeks. Topic filters are useful when school homework calls for one particular idea.

This expansion adds **32 original lessons and 96 challenges per grade**. Grade 4 has 46 available activities including earlier review; Grade 5 has 35. Each new lesson has an introduction, three practice challenges, three bounded replay variants, and an “Our lesson” guide with a goal, prerequisite, independent worked example, explanation prompt, and review prompt. Replaying different numbers is practice, rather than a new lesson.

## Make the model useful

- **Keep the given quantity fixed.** Green or blue models describe the problem. Brown trial drawings show a proposed answer separately. Change the answer and compare before checking.
- **Name the unit.** One whole has a consistent size in fraction bars and decimal grids. Area uses square units, volume uses cubic units, and conversions preserve the measured quantity.
- **Connect a picture to a written method.** For multiplication, name each partial rectangle. For division, multiply a proposed quotient by the divisor, then examine what remains. For fractions, rename pieces without changing their size in total.
- **Explain a fresh example.** Ask what stayed the same, why a result is reasonable, and how to check it another way. Revisit earlier units freely; there are no prerequisite locks or speed penalties.

Numeric responses can use bounded controls; fraction and decimal entry also work where offered. Reading settings, keyboard controls, free hints, calm motion, saved progress, and optional companion rewards remain available. Take breaks whenever useful.

These activities, examples, diagrams, and stories are independently authored Math Atlas content. Official planning references include the [Grade 4 overview](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/4/introduction/), [Grade 4 fractions](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/4/NF/), [Grade 5 overview](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/5/introduction/), [Grade 5 fractions](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/5/NF/), and [Grade 5 measurement and data](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/5/MD/). Standards codes indicate topic connections to review. They do not certify complete coverage or alignment with a particular school program.

The Grade 5 fraction-division path focuses on the unit-fraction cases expected at this level. General fraction-by-fraction division belongs to later development. These are growing practice companions, rather than 180 daily lesson plans, a mastery assessment, or a replacement for school instruction.
