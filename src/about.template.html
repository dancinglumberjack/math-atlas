Why this exists

Math makes more sense
when you can see it.

Math Atlas is a growing, open-source collection of interactive school math helpers. It began with a simple goal: help a student move beyond a memorized rule to understand what the rule means.

An adventure that grows with you

Play with an idea. Then see the math.

Choose a learning stage from Pre-K to introductory college explorations. Younger children count, combine and remove objects, match shapes, and set clocks through game interactions. Older students connect their answers with changing models, equations, and graphs.

Original woodland friends bring gentle humor to early adventures. The presentation becomes more mature for later stages, and a quieter view is always available. Missions have a clear finish, free hints, and no speed penalties. Collect pretend coins to choose pets, habitats, cases, keychain charms, and imaginary snacks. Pets stay safe when you take a break.

This is a growing set of selected explorations, not a complete Pre-K-to-college curriculum. Journey and collection data stay in this browser; there are no accounts or classroom tracking services.

Find what you are learning at school

A grade. A topic. One little discovery.

The adventure has 99 missions with 297 short challenges. Choose a grade, then a topic. Grade 3 has separate multiplication and division paths: equal groups, arrays, skip-counting, useful fact strategies, fair shares, missing factors, and checking an answer by multiplying back.

Division answers can be built right in the picture. Large + and − buttons add or remove equal rows, or change the share in every row. The total changes with each tap; checking uses the built model, without requiring a typed number.

Grade labels are starting points, and topics can overlap grades. High-school sequences vary. The activities link to relevant Common Core topic codes where useful; this growing collection does not cover every standard or replace a complete course.

A small learning loop

How to use Math Atlas

  1. Find one idea. Search the library or choose a collection, from number sense to advanced explorations. Read the lesson goal and check “Helpful to know.”
  2. Make a prediction. Before moving a control, ask yourself what you expect to change—and what should stay the same.
  3. Explore the model. Move one slider at a time. On selected geometry lessons, compare flat and isometric views, enlarge the drawing, or separate the prism’s layers.
  4. Work through the reasoning. Reveal the example one step at a time. Use the prerequisite links or quick math words if a term is unfamiliar.
  5. Try it yourself. Switch to practice. Use a hint, the scientific calculator, or “See the reasoning” when needed. A correct check marks the lesson practiced; it does not certify mastery.
  6. Try another example. Explain why the result makes sense. Use “New challenge” to change the values, or return later with your place saved.

Make the screen work for you

Reading and focus

Clear sans-serif type, larger everyday text, spacious lines, and one worked step at a time are already on. Open Reading support or Adjust reading to change typeface, text size, spacing, page color, contrast, motion, and the optional reading guide. Extra reading and focus presets are available. These are personal preferences; different readers find different settings useful.

Focus support reduces clutter and can show one worked step at a time.

Tools that support reasoning

The scientific calculator supports degree/radian modes, powers, roots, logarithms, trigonometry, and a short calculation history. Its result can be copied into a practice answer; checking remains a separate action.

Worked steps and model settings are remembered in the same browser. There is no student account or cross-device sync. Clearing browser site data removes those saved choices.

Bring a problem from school

The equation entry accepts ax + b = c, using whole-number a from 1–6, b from −6–6, and c from −20–30. For example, 3x + 4 = 16 opens a worked lesson with that equation.

This entry is a bounded equation helper, not an unrestricted solver. For other topics, choose the appropriate lesson and adjust its controls. Practice accepts numeric answers, fractions such as 3/4, and scientific notation such as 3e-5.

Built to grow carefully

What’s here—and what comes next

There are 80 interactive lessons in nine collections: number sense, ratios and percents, pre-algebra, Algebra I, geometry, Algebra II, trigonometry, statistics and probability, and advanced explorations. Six lessons offer Anatomy-style spatial models.

This edition explores major topics; it is not a complete, certified standards-aligned curriculum. Full proof and construction sequences, extended word-problem coaching, comprehensive inference, and deeper assessments still need development.

Math Atlas uses browser-local storage for progress and preferences. This version does not include built-in analytics or send practice answers to an application server. The hosting provider and browser may still process ordinary requests.
